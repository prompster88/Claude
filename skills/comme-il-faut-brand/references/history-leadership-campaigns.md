# Comme Il Faut — History, Leadership & Campaign Archive

> Last verified: August 2026, from public sources. Use this for brand storytelling, "about" copy,
> press replies, anniversary content, and anything that needs the brand's real record — not a vibe.

---

## 1. Origin & name

- **Founded 1987** by **Sybil Goldfiner (סיביל גולדפיינר)** together with **Carole Godin (קרול גודין)**,
  starting out as a **home dressmaking business (תופרת בית)**. Not a corporate launch — a kitchen-table start.
- **The name** is the French expression *comme il faut* — "as it should be" — taken from
  **Chekhov's "The Seagull."**
- **The feminist reframe:** the brand rewrote the phrase's meaning to
  **"as it should be = as *you* want it"** (*כמו שצריך = כמו שאת רוצה*).
  This is the single most useful sentence in the brand's arsenal. Use it.
- **Legal entity:** COMME IL FAUT LTD. / קום איל פו בע"מ, company no. **511906166**.

## 2. The 2000 turn — why it became political

After years in Israeli fashion, the founders kept seeing the same thing: women of every age, size and
body type in serious conflict with their own bodies, avoiding the mirror. **In 2000 they decided to use
the brand's commercial power to push a feminist agenda** — and the fashion house has been formally
committed to a feminist worldview ever since. Fashion is the vehicle; the message is the point.

**Slow fashion** is the production doctrine: carefully made, quality garments produced under fair
conditions, from good materials, with environmental consideration — explicitly positioned as part of the
international movement *against* mass production built on cheap exploited labour.

## 4. The new design chapter — Daniel Cohen, SS26

- Joined after **six years in Hed Mayner's studio** (international menswear label).
- **Bezalel** graduate — tailoring & fashion department.
- Started by going into **the brand's archive**: cuts, campaigns and texts going back to 1987.
- **Debut collection: Spring/Summer 2026** — roughly **20 models**, **produced in Portugal**,
  launched at the Levontin flagship.
- Signature pieces reported: **wide-leg pleated jeans**, a **grey wool blazer with a sleeve seam**,
  **coloured linen dresses**, **poplin striped shirts**.
- Her stated philosophy: *"אני מעצבת בגדים והייתי רוצה שתהיה להם השפעה על תנועת הגוף ועל ההופעה ברחוב.
  בגדים מחויטים אכן מפסלים את התנועה שלך."* — clothes as sculpture for how a woman moves in the street.
- Press angle used at launch: **"למצוא את הסוד שיש בחושניות"** — finding the secret inside sensuality.

## 5. Buying strategy — courting a younger customer

The new chapter is also a **buying** strategy, not only a design one: importing labels that a younger
customer already wants. Named in press: **Miista** (Spain/UK — back for a second season with a
punk-leaning line), the Korean label **aarahee**, plus **MOMA**, **Officine Creative** and a Dover Street
Market house label. In total roughly **20 imported brands from Italy, Japan and Spain** — scarves,
bags, hats, belts, accessories — all selected on quality and uncompromising design.

## 6. Circularity — "מעבירות" (Ma'avirot), launched 2008

- Customers bring back **used Comme Il Faut garments** and receive a **credit (~₪150)** toward the new collection.
- Returned garments have been channelled to the **"Ishit" (אישית) association**, with sale proceeds funding
  **empowerment and professional-advancement courses for women**.
- The brand has also reported donating **10% of secondhand proceeds to the Green Network** (from 2022).
- **COMME IL FAUT VINTAGE** began as a dedicated secondhand store inside the Port complex —
  collector pieces gathered from customers and from the brand archive. That function now lives at the
  **Kibbutz Galuyot 29 studio** and on the site's **Vintage** page.
- Slogan asset in active use: **"FUCK FAST FASHION"** — used as physical signage, including at the Haifa pop-up.

## 8. Brand risk — the criticism, stated honestly

Know these before writing anything political or price-related. They are the actual counter-arguments
the brand meets in the wild:

1. **"Elitist."** Recurring critique: expensive clothes for women with excessive purchasing power.
   **The brand's own answer:** prices are not inflated — *"אלפי שקלים לפריט? כשתקני בזול תדעי שמישהו משלם ביוקר"*
   (when you buy cheap, know that someone else is paying dearly). The biggest fortunes in fashion belong to
   cheap-fashion shareholders. They would like to sell for less; slow fashion with real materials makes it impossible.
   And there **is** an accessible route: the outlet and the deep seasonal sales.
2. **"Archaic and disconnected."** TimeOut ran an explicit piece headlined that the campaign was
   *"ארכאי ומנותק כמו המותג עצמו."* This critique — that the brand speaks to an older, affluent, shrinking
   audience — is precisely what the Levontin move, Daniel Cohen, and the younger imported labels are answering.
3. **Right-wing backlash on the political work.** Israel Hayom ran *"קום איל פו מסיתה נגד ההתיישבות ביו״ש"*
   (incitement). The Gaza-hunger campaign drew condemnation. Expect hostile comments on every political post.
   **Never apologise for the brand's positions in copy** — but do not pick fights in comment replies either.
   The campaign is the statement; the comment section is not.

## 9. Storytelling assets you can draw on

- 1987. A dressmaker's table. Two women. Still independent, still family-run, nearly 40 years on.
- Chekhov → "as it should be" → **"as you want it."**
- The archive at Kibbutz Galuyot: every garment since 1987, and a new designer who started by reading it.
- 20 years at the Port, ended by rising rent and fast fashion moving in — the exact forces the brand exists to oppose.
- A heritage building on Levontin with a gallery and a courtyard: retail that is also a cultural venue.
- Empty pots. Chefs. Three languages.
- Ma'avirot: your old coat comes back, gets sold, and pays for another woman's training.
