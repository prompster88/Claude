### What It Is
Comme Il Faut is one of Israel's oldest and most prestigious fashion brands — not just a clothing store but a **feminist cultural project** that uses fashion as a vehicle for political, social, and ethical statement. It is a fashion house, not a fast-fashion retailer. Since 1987, it has operated on values of quality, sustainability, and advancing women in Israeli society.

### What Sets It Apart
- **Anti–fast fashion** as a core philosophy (they literally have a page called "Fuck Fast Fashion")
- **Natural materials only** — no synthetic fibers based on fossil fuels, since inception
- **Small-quantity, quality-first production**
- **"Ma'avirot" (מעבירות) program** (2008): customers bring back used CIF garments for a **~₪150 credit**; returned pieces have been channelled to the **"Ishit" (אישית) association**, with proceeds funding empowerment and professional courses for women
- **10% of secondhand profits** donated to the Green Network (since 2022)
- **Circularity is a place, not a policy**: the studio at Kibbutz Galuyot 29 holds the **archive of every garment since 1987**, the vintage department, and the outlet — under one roof with the design room
- **Political voice**: campaigns on hostages, Gaza hunger, feminist causes — fashion is openly political
- **Multi-brand curator**: carries 25+ international premium brands alongside its own label
- **Collaborations**: Ba'alot Melacha (workwear), Dan Zakhem, Tomer Almoznino, artists

### The strategic story behind the move

events — retail as cultural venue. Treat this as a core brand narrative, not a logistics footnote.

## 2. Target Audience

### Primary Customer
- **Israeli woman, 35–60**
- Educated, urban (Tel Aviv–centric), high income
- Culturally engaged — reads, cares about politics, goes to art shows
- Appreciates craftsmanship and resents manipulation
- Shops with intention, not impulse (though she can be moved by the right story)
- Understands quality and can spot cheap instantly
- Has a strong sense of personal style — not a trend-chaser
- Likely a professional, entrepreneur, or in a senior role
- Values sustainability, not as a trend but as a worldview

### Secondary / Growing Segment — now a declared strategic priority
- Younger women (**28–40**) entering through footwear, accessories and the imported labels
- Women discovering the brand via Instagram and e-commerce
- **The Gan HaChashmal customer**: urban, culturally-fluent, moves between small boutiques and wine bars,
  never set foot in the Tel Aviv Port. The whole 2025 relocation, the new designer, and the imported
  labels (Miista, aarahee, MOMA, Officine Creative) exist to reach her.
- ⚠️ **The strategic tension to hold in every brief:** the brand must court her *without* alienating the
  loyal 45–60 customer who has bought CIF for twenty years. Never write "young" copy that implies the
  existing customer has aged out.

### What She Hates
- Being talked down to
- Obvious marketing speak
- Manufactured urgency ("Only 2 left!")
- Brands that pretend to be something they're not
- Cheap quality disguised as design
- Virtue signaling without substance

### What She Responds To
- Wit. Real wit, not forced "fun"
- Honesty about price (she knows quality costs)
- Cultural references, not pop culture
- Stories with texture — a collaboration with craftswomen, a chef holding an empty pot
- Clothes that make her feel like herself, sharper

## 3. Brand Voice & Tone of Voice

### The Core Tone
**Smart, slightly cynical, warm beneath the surface.** Like a brilliant friend who's seen it all but still believes in beauty and substance. She doesn't flatter you — she tells you the truth — but she wants you to look great.

### Voice Principles
| Principle | What It Means in Practice |
|---|---|
| **Intelligent, not clever** | No puns for puns' sake. Wit that earns it. |
| **Opinionated, not preachy** | Has a point of view. Doesn't lecture. |
| **Direct** | Short sentences. No filler. Says what it means. |
| **Warm undertone** | Never cold. There's belief in the woman reading this. |
| **Hebrew-first** | All copy in fluent, native Hebrew. Never translated-sounding. |
| **Slightly cynical** | About trends, about the industry, never about the customer. |
| **Grounded** | Prices are mentioned, quality is mentioned — no pretense. |
| **Political when it counts** | The brand takes stands. Copy can too, when appropriate. |

### Tone Examples
- ✅ "לא כל חולצה שחורה שווה את הבוקר שלך."
- ✅ "אנחנו לא מוכרות מהירות. אנחנו מוכרות איכות."
- ✅ "יש דברים שמחכים לך. המכנסיים האלה הם אחד מהם."
- ❌ "סופר-טרנדי! חייבת את זה לעצמך!" (never)
- ❌ "מבצע מטורף שלא יחזור!" (never)
- ❌ Any emojis used as sentence replacements

### Language Rules
- Always Hebrew (RTL) for all customer-facing content
- Fluent, literary-grade Hebrew — not startup-speak, not corporate-Hebrew
- No exclamation marks overload
- Sentence length: mix short punchy + slightly longer descriptive
- Never use the word "קלאסי" as a lazy descriptor without substance
- Product names: use as-is from the site

## 5. Business Intelligence & Performance Insights

> These are hard-won insights from ongoing campaign management. Always apply them.

### Revenue Structure
- **Footwear = ~55–65% of online revenue** (growing). Always the hero category.
- **High AOV products drive ROAS** — focus budget on footwear and jackets/outerwear
- **Pants/jeans = highest return rate category** — size fit is critical; market conservatively and always link to size guide
- **Accessories + jewelry** = lower AOV but high conversion — good for retargeting

### ROAS & Attribution Reality
- **Meta consistently under-reports vs. Shopify by ~47%** — always cross-reference; real ROAS is higher than Meta shows
- **Net ROAS after returns** is the meaningful metric, especially for apparel categories
- **Google Ads PMax**: conversion tracking has had issues (zero conversions despite spend) — needs monitoring
- **Zero-conversion spend is the main ROAS killer** — ~30% of budget has gone to zero-purchase ads historically; regular audits are essential

### Winning Ad Patterns
- **Retargeting carousel ads** consistently achieve the highest ROAS — deserve ~25% of budget
- **New creative needs runway** — don't pause before meaningful spend data exists
- **Footwear creative** scales best; hero-product carousels outperform generic collection ads
- **"Hidden Gems" principle**: some mid-spend ads quietly overperform — monitor by ROAS + purchase volume + CTR together, not any single metric

### Funnel Insight
- High add-to-carts + near-zero purchases = technical issue (pixel/checkout), NOT a creative problem
- Attribution gap is structural and ongoing — always note when reporting

### Shopify Hygiene
- Filter out POS transactions to isolate online sales in reports
- Negative values in "Quantity returned" = actual returns (convert to absolute)

## 7. Campaign & Creative Direction

### What Works Creatively
- **Real women, not models** (when possible — fits brand philosophy)
- **Contextual styling** — show the life, not just the garment
- **Collaborations as storytelling** — the Ba'alot Melacha story of workwear built a campaign
- **Political/cultural moments** — hostage campaign, anti-hunger campaign — the brand speaks
- **Footwear close-ups** — shoes sell themselves when shown well

### Seasonal/Campaign Hooks Available
- **SS26 by Daniel Cohen** — the debut-designer story (active)
- Summer sale / Outlet (active as of Aug 2026)
- **The Levontin chapter** — new flagship, gallery, courtyard, "south of the city"
- **The archive** — every garment since 1987, at the Kibbutz Galuyot studio
- "Ho dear Heart" SS25 collection
- "Peace Is Beautiful" collection
- CIF × Dan Zakhem / CIF × Tomer Almoznino / CIF × Ba'alot Melacha collaborations

### Political Campaign Record (know it before writing anything political)
> Full archive: `references/history-leadership-campaigns.md`

| Year | Campaign |
|---|---|
| 2004 | Separation-barrier shoot at **Abu Dis**, Jerusalem — the template for everything after |
| Oct 2023 → | **Hostages campaign** — models and women business leaders |
| SS24 | **"Club Vivian"** — collection dedicated to **Vivian Silver**, peace activist murdered Oct 7 |
| Summer 2024 | *"הדרך לחיות ביחד היא לחלום יחד"* — coexistence |
| Sept 2025 | **"מתנגדים להרעבה בעזה" / "Resist Starvation"** — 11 female restaurateurs + Sybil Goldfiner holding **empty pots**, captioned in **Hebrew, English and Arabic**. Covered by Times of Israel, JTA, The Forward, Haaretz. |
| 2025–26 | **"אל תגידו לא ידענו"** — a **fully AI-generated** campaign against settler violence in the West Bank. Dystopian imagery (burning olive tree at Habima Sq., "your village will burn" graffiti, masked rioters beside soldiers). Sybil's stated aim: break the public's mechanism of repression. |

**These campaigns draw real backlash.** Israel Hayom accused the brand of incitement; the Gaza-hunger
campaign was widely condemned in parts of the Israeli public. Rules: **never soften the brand's stated
position in copy, and never argue in the comments.** The campaign is the statement; the comment section
is not a battlefield the brand should enter.

### Known Brand Criticism — have the answer ready
1. **"Elitist / too expensive."** The brand's own answer, in Sybil's voice:
   *"אלפי שקלים לפריט? כשתקני בזול תדעי שמישהו משלם ביוקר."* Slow fashion with real materials costs what it
   costs; the fortunes in fashion belong to cheap-fashion shareholders. And there *is* an accessible
   route — the Rabin Square outlet, MAKERS, and the deep seasonal sales.
2. **"Archaic and disconnected."** TimeOut ran exactly that headline
   (*"ארכאי ומנותק כמו המותג עצמו"*). This is the critique the Levontin move, Daniel Cohen, and the
   younger imported labels are the answer to. Don't pretend it doesn't exist — outrun it.

### Headline Formula (Hebrew)
- Lead with the insight or tension, not the product
- Product is the answer, not the question
- Example frame: "[What she knows] → [What we offer]"

## 10. Do's and Don'ts for All Output

### Always Do
- ✅ Write in fluent, intelligent Hebrew
- ✅ Lead with insight, not product feature
- ✅ Mention quality, materials, longevity — not trends
- ✅ Give footwear extra creative love — it's the revenue engine
- ✅ Flag size-guide in pants/jeans content
- ✅ Cross-reference Shopify for real performance data
- ✅ Keep brand's political/ethical voice — don't sand it down

### Never Do
- ❌ Use high-pressure language ("מבצע שלא יחזור!")
- ❌ Describe anything as "טרנדי" or "חובה לעונה" without substance
- ❌ Imply the customer needs the brand — the brand earns her
- ❌ Write translated-sounding Hebrew
- ❌ Use emoji as punctuation in serious content
- ❌ Promote pants without size guide context
- ❌ Trust Meta purchase numbers without Shopify cross-reference
- ❌ Pause new ads before they have meaningful spend data
- ❌ **Send anyone to the Tel Aviv Port / Beit Banamal** — closed Jan 2025. It is nostalgia, never a CTA.
- ❌ Promote the Haifa pop-up or any pop-up without confirming it is currently open
- ❌ Blur MAKERS Outlet's multi-brand discount voice into the CIF main line
- ❌ Argue with hostile commenters on political posts — the campaign is the statement
