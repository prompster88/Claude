## 1. How Israeli media deals really work

- **Rate cards (מחירון) are an opening position.** Nearly every serious buy is done at a deep discount off rate card, via agency annual deals (הסכמים שנתיים) plus campaign-level negotiation. Only the net number matters.
- **Agencies buy in bulk.** Large media buying houses negotiate annual volume with the TV groups, publishers and platforms; small advertisers ride on those terms. Ask your agency which terms you get and whether any vendor benefit (bonus inventory, rebates/"החזרים", incentive trips, etc.) flows back to you. Full transparency should be a clause in the agency agreement.
- **Inventory is perishable.** Unsold airtime/impressions this week are worth zero to the vendor next week. That's your leverage: flexibility on dates and placement buys discount.
- **Seasonality drives price.** Demand peaks before Rosh Hashana/Tishrei, Q4 (Black Friday/November, end-of-year), and pre-Pesach; it softens in the summer and in the weeks right after holidays and during security escalations. Election periods (party ads are regulated separately on TV/radio but digital demand spikes) can tighten inventory.
- **Relationships matter.** Israeli media is a small market; sales reps remember who's reliable. A client who pays on time, sends material on time and doesn't cancel last minute gets better terms next time — use it as a card.

## 2. Levers by medium

### TV
- Net CPP on the client target (not on households).
- % prime time (typically 20:00–23:00) — push for a guaranteed minimum.
- Positioning: first/last in break (often a surcharge of 10–25% — ask for it free or at a reduced surcharge).
- Guaranteed GRPs with make-goods if under-delivered on post-buy (Kantar/ועדת המדרוג data).
- Spot length mix: 15" cutdowns stretch budget; ask for pricing at a true pro-rata (vendors often charge 15" at 60–70% of 30").
- Sponsorship billboards (חסויות) in relevant shows (lifestyle, health, cooking, women's talk shows) — often better value for niche targets than spots.
- Content integration / product placement: price each element; ensure it's compliant (disclosure rules).
- Bonus airtime: demand it is on-target and at least X% in prime.

### Radio
- Drive-time share, guaranteed number of spots per day part.
- Host-read / live mentions (often more effective, check regulatory limits on health claims).
- Sponsorship of segments (weather, traffic, health segment).
- Regional stations let you concentrate on geography cheaply.

### Digital publishers (Ynet, Mako, Walla, Haaretz, etc.)
- Net CPM on viewable impressions; viewability ≥70% guarantee.
- Frequency cap per user.
- Homepage takeovers are priced for ego — negotiate hard or skip.
- Sponsored content: guaranteed page views/readers, distribution budget included, time-on-page reporting, content lifespan on site, SEO rights, right to reuse the content on your own channels.
- Health/women's verticals and newsletters are often under-sold — good value for a specific target.

### Social / programmatic
- Auction prices aren't negotiable; the **agency fee** (% of spend or retainer), **ops quality**, **creative production** and **reporting** are.
- Ask for test budgets, clear learning plan, and weekly optimization reports.

### OOH / DOOH
- Location list with traffic data and photos; exclude weak faces.
- Price per face per week, and share of loop (1/6 vs 1/10) on digital screens.
- Free extension weeks when the next advertiser isn't booked ("עודפים").
- Production and installation costs — often a hidden 10–20%.

### Influencers / podcasts
- Price per story/reel/post with insights (last 30 days reach average) in writing.
- Usage rights (can you boost it as an ad? for how long?), exclusivity only if paid for.
- Performance components: personal promo code, affiliate/bonus per sign-up.
- Disclosure plan (#פרסומת / "שיתוף פעולה בתשלום" label) — mandatory.

## 3. Tactics

1. **Anchor with data, not feelings.** "Our net CPP target for W40–60 is X based on our last flight and market range; you're at 1.4X."
2. **Unbundle.** Ask for the price of each element separately. Bundles hide weak items.
3. **Competitive tension.** Always have a credible alternative (another channel, another publisher, or moving budget to digital). Mention it, don't bluff about numbers you don't have.
4. **Give to get.** Every concession you ask for, offer something cheap for you: date flexibility, fill-in inventory, testimonial/case study, faster payment, multi-flight commitment *with exit clauses*.
5. **Ask for the "not-for-sale" value.** Editorial mentions, newsletter placements, social amplification of the sponsored content, event invites — low marginal cost to vendor, real value to you.
6. **Timing.** Push at the end of the vendor's month/quarter; book early for peak seasons (lock rates), book late (last-minute) for soft periods.
7. **Test before you commit.** Propose a smaller first flight with a pre-agreed price for the scale-up if KPIs are met.
8. **Get it in writing.** Every verbal promise ("we'll give you a bonus", "we'll put you first in break") must appear in the insertion order (הזמנת עבודה / IO).
9. **Walk-away is real.** Know your BATNA (best alternative) before the meeting.

## 4. Contract / IO clauses to insist on

- Net price, all fees, and VAT treatment explicit.
- Delivery guarantee (GRPs/impressions/views) + make-good mechanism + measurement source (Kantar / Google Ad Manager / third-party verification such as IAS/DV, Ifat for tracking).
- Pre-emption & force majeure clause covering emergency broadcasts and security situations, with the right to postpone or cancel without penalty and credit for unused inventory.
- Creative approval & swap rights; right to pull creative if context becomes inappropriate (e.g. placement next to war coverage).
- Cancellation terms (e.g. free until 14 days before airing, partial after).
- Reporting: format, frequency, raw data.
- Payment terms (שוטף + X).
- For content/influencers: usage rights, approval rights, disclosure responsibility, non-disparagement, removal on breach.
- Agency agreement: full transparency of rebates/bonuses; ownership of ad accounts and data by the client.

## 5. Hebrew phrasing bank

- "אנחנו עובדים על בסיס נטו-נטו. תשלחו לי בבקשה את העלות לנקודת רייטינג על קהל היעד שלנו – נשים 40–60 – ולא על משקי בית."
- "ההצעה שלכם גבוהה בכ־X% ממה שאנחנו רואים בשוק לאותו פורמט ולאותה עונה. איפה אתם יכולים להתגמש?"
- "אני צריך לראות פירוק של החבילה – מחיר לכל רכיב בנפרד."
- "הבונוס מעניין, אבל רק אם הוא בפריים ועל קהל היעד. אחרת הוא לא שווה לנו כלום."
- "אנחנו גמישים בתאריכים – אם יש לכם שבועות חלשים באוקטובר–נובמבר, בואו נדבר על מחיר בהתאם."
- "אני מבקש סעיף שמבטיח פיצוי (make-good) אם לא עומדים באספקה, ואפשרות להקפיא/לדחות בלי קנס במקרה של מצב ביטחוני או שידורים מיוחדים."
- "אנחנו מתחילים בפיילוט. אם נעמוד ביעדים – יש לנו תקציב המשך, ואני רוצה לקבע כבר עכשיו את המחיר להמשך."
- "יש לנו הצעה מתחרה. אני מעדיף לעבוד איתכם, אבל אתם צריכים להתקרב ל־X."
- "תשלחו בבקשה צילומי מסך של תובנות (Insights) מ־30 הימים האחרונים – חשיפה ממוצעת לסטורי ולריל."
- "מה אתם יכולים להוסיף שלא עולה לכם כסף – ניוזלטר, אזכור ברשתות, הפצה של הכתבה?"

## 6. Direct buy vs. agency

- **Agency pros:** access to annual-deal pricing, buying clout, operational capacity, post-buy analysis.
- **Agency cons:** fee (commonly a % of media or a retainer), possible conflicts of interest via vendor incentives, small clients get junior attention.
- **Direct:** often fine for digital performance (Meta/Google/TikTok), influencers, niche publishers, podcasts and small OOH. TV direct buying for a small advertiser usually yields worse prices than via a buying house.
- **Hybrid:** agency for TV/radio/OOH; in-house or boutique for performance & influencers.
